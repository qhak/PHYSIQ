What FFMI actually is

FFMI is like BMI's more honest cousin. BMI just divides your weight by your height — it can't tell a bodybuilder from someone carrying extra fat, because it doesn't know what the weight is. FFMI strips out body fat first, so what's left is a number that actually tracks muscle mass relative to your height.

It comes from a 1995 study (Kouri et al.) that measured drug-free and steroid-using bodybuilders and found a rough ceiling in the natural group's FFMI scores — which is where the "25 is about the natural limit" figure you'll see everywhere originally came from.

What the number means

<18Below average — little visible muscular development yet.
18–20Average — typical for someone with little to no training history.
20–22Muscular — visible, consistent training shows here.
22–23Advanced — clearly built, years of serious training.
23–25Excellent — near the upper range most often cited for natural training.
25+Exceptional — rare. The figure most commonly referenced as an approximate natural ceiling — not a hard rule.
These bands (and the "25" figure especially) are commonly cited reference points from older research and decades of anecdotal data — not a scientifically proven hard limit. Genetics, frame size, limb length, and training history all move where any individual actually lands. Treat FFMI as a rough compass, not a verdict.

Why it needs your body fat %

FFMI can't work off weight alone — it needs to know how much of that weight is actually muscle versus fat. If you don't know your body fat percentage precisely, a rough estimate is fine for this calculator; the number moves slowly with small errors in body fat.

Questions

What is FFMI?
FFMI stands for Fat-Free Mass Index. It measures how much muscle mass you carry relative to your height, similar to how BMI relates weight to height — except FFMI strips out body fat first, so it actually reflects muscle rather than being thrown off by it.
What is a good FFMI?
Roughly: under 18 is untrained, 18-20 is average, 20-22 is visibly muscular from consistent training, 22-23 is advanced, and 23-25 is excellent. Around 25 is the figure most commonly cited in lifting communities as a rough natural ceiling, though genetics vary a lot and this is a reference point, not a hard rule.
Is 25 FFMI actually the natural limit?
No single number is a hard limit — it depends heavily on individual genetics, frame, and training history. 25 is simply the figure most often cited as an approximate upper range for drug-free lifters based on older research and decades of anecdotal data, not a scientifically proven ceiling every natural lifter will hit.
Why does FFMI need body fat percentage?
FFMI is calculated from fat-free mass, so it needs to know how much of your weight is fat versus everything else. Without a body fat estimate, the calculator can't separate muscle mass from total weight.
