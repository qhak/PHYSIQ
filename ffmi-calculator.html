What FFMI measures

Fat-free mass is everything in body weight other than fat mass. It includes skeletal muscle, water, bone and organs. FFMI expresses that estimated mass relative to height; it cannot isolate how many kilograms of muscle you carry.

A change in FFMI can reflect a change in weight or the body-fat input. It does not, by itself, establish muscle gain, muscle loss, health or substance use.

The formula

Two steps: take the fat out of your weight, then divide by height squared. BMI uses total weight divided by height squared; FFMI uses estimated fat-free mass instead.

Fat-free mass
weight (kg) × (1 − body fat % ÷ 100)
FFMI
fat-free mass ÷ height (m) ²
Normalized FFMI
FFMI + 6.1 × (1.8 − height in m)
Worked example. 80 kg at 15% body fat, 1.75 m tall. Fat-free mass is 80 × 0.85 = 68 kg. FFMI is 68 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.2. Then normalized: 22.2 + 6.1 × (1.8 − 1.75) = 22.5.
Our height convention: CutRank retains 6.1 × (1.80 − height in metres). Kouri and colleagues' original abstract reports 6.3. The correction changes the index, not your actual body composition. At 1.80 m it is zero. Compare the conventions.

Interpret the result without a training grade

The result is an index in kg/m², not a percentile or a rating of your physique. A single value cannot establish how long someone has trained, how strong they are, or whether their diet should change.

The often-quoted normalized FFMI of 25 comes from Kouri and colleagues' 1995 sample of male athletes. It is not a universal natural ceiling or a doping test, and the study is not a reference scale for everyone. Read the original study.

How much does an uncertain input matter?

For the 80 kg, 1.75 m example, entering 15% body fat gives raw FFMI 22.2. Entering 10% gives 23.5; entering 20% gives 20.9. That spread comes entirely from the input, with no change in measured weight or height.

Try plausible inputs on either side of your estimate to see the sensitivity. This is a scenario comparison, not a confidence interval. Record your input method and use comparable conditions for later checks.

Questions

What is FFMI?
FFMI is fat-free mass in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. Fat-free mass includes muscle, water, bone and organs; it is not a direct measurement of skeletal muscle.
What is a good FFMI?
There is no universal target or validated training grade here. Interpretation depends on the input method and the population being compared. This calculator reports an index, not health, strength or training history.
Is 25 FFMI the natural limit?
No. The often-quoted value comes from a particular male-athlete study and is not a universal biological ceiling. FFMI cannot establish whether an individual has used steroids.
Why does FFMI need body fat percentage?
The calculator uses body-fat percentage to estimate how much total weight is fat-free mass. An uncertain body-fat input produces an uncertain FFMI output.
Why does CutRank use 6.1 for normalized FFMI?
This calculator retains CutRank's existing 6.1 height-correction convention. The original Kouri study abstract reports 6.3. Record the coefficient when comparing results; normalization does not correct body-fat measurement error.
