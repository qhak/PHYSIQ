What tier is my physique?

CutRank uses six photo-based grades: S, A, B, C, D and E, from exceptional visible development to little visible muscular development. The scale describes what appears in a photo; it cannot establish someone's training history, and pose, lighting and which areas are visible affect the result.

The quick self-check

Use these as broad descriptions of visible muscular development, not estimates of training experience or a judgment of the person in the photo.

ELittle visible muscular development. The photo shows few clear signs of muscular size or shape.
DSome visible development. A small amount of muscular size or shape is apparent in the areas shown.
CDeveloping visible base. Muscular development is apparent, with substantial room for development across visible areas.
BEstablished visible base. Muscular development is clear, with visible differences between stronger and less-developed areas.
AAdvanced visible development. Muscular size and shape are substantial across the visible physique. More on A-tier.
SExceptional visible development. Muscular size, thickness and proportion stand out at the top of CutRank's scale. More on S-tier.
This self-check is only a rough guide. A one-photo grade is affected by pose, lighting, clothing and which areas are visible. A photo cannot show someone's training history, and a grade is not a measure of health or personal worth.

Why self-assessment can vary

Comparison group

Your reference changes the read

Comparing yourself with elite or highly selected images online gives a different impression from using the definitions in this page's rubric.

Use the same written criteria and comparable photos over time, and remember that this scale is CutRank's editorial convention rather than a population norm.

Photo conditions

Small changes affect appearance

Lighting, angle, pose, clothing and a temporary pump can change what is visible in an image.

Record those conditions and compare like with like. A photo grade describes that image; it does not establish an objective or permanent tier.

Getting an outside read

A second opinion can add another perspective, but a person or model may still use different standards and be affected by the same photo conditions.

Moving up

  • Use more than the grade — training records, goals, recovery and consistent photos provide context a single image cannot.
  • Review specific areas cautiously — visible differences between muscle groups can suggest questions to explore, but the photo may not show every area clearly.
  • Appearance is multi-factor — pose, lighting, proportions, muscular development and conditioning can all affect a photo's impression. These are rubric considerations, not a universal aesthetic rule.

What this isn't

Tiers are a grading convention, not a medical or scientific classification, and they describe visible muscular development only — not health, fitness, strength or worth. CutRank reads one photo and returns an AI estimate rather than a measurement; lighting, pose, pump and camera angle all move the result. Under-18, explicit, multi-person and unreadable photos are refused rather than graded. 18+ only.

Questions

What tier is my physique?
CutRank's S–E scale describes visible muscular development in a photo, from little visible development to exceptional development. It cannot establish training history, health or personal worth. Lighting, pose and which areas are visible affect a photo-based grade.
How accurate is judging your own physique?
Self-assessment depends on the comparison group, familiarity with your own appearance and photo conditions. Use the same rubric and comparable photos over time; the result remains a subjective estimate, not a measurement.
What tier is the average gym goer?
There is no reliable population distribution for CutRank's tiers in the information presented here. The grades are an editorial photo-based scale, and the photo cannot show training history.
How do I move up a tier?
The grade alone cannot prescribe a plan. Consider your training goals and records, recovery and any consistently observed areas you want to develop. A photo-based score can be one prompt for reflection, not a substitute for individualized coaching.
Do tiers account for body fat?
A photo grade describes visible muscular development, so lighting, pose and how clearly areas show can affect the result. The first-angle grade does not include a body-fat estimate, and a photo cannot measure body fat precisely. For measurement options and their limits, see the body-fat measurement guide.
