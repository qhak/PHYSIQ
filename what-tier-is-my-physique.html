Use the result as a starting point

  • Check the angle. Which muscles are visible, and which are hidden? Do not read one angle as a whole-body assessment.
  • Read the feedback. A specific visible finding is more useful than the letter alone. Compare it with your training goals.
  • Keep comparisons fair. Use similar pose, lighting, camera distance and framing. Small score differences can reflect uncertainty.
  • Avoid automatic diet decisions. A grade cannot establish calorie needs or whether a bulk or cut is appropriate.

How the bands relate to your card

The internal score maps to the letter using the bands below. CutRank's displayed 1–10 comparison score uses a separate mapping; it is not the S–E score divided by ten or a validated population percentile.

For details about individual bands, read S-tier and A-tier. For later checks, use the progress-photo guide.

CutRank's S–E rubric

These are CutRank's internal 0–100 grade bands. The labels are product shorthand for visible development, not clinical categories or measured population percentiles. The card's 1–10 comparison score is a separate product scale.

SElite · 90–100 — Exceptional visible development within CutRank's scale.
AAdvanced · 75–89 — Strong visible development, one band below S.
BExperienced · 61–74 — A developed visible base with areas to improve.
CDeveloping · 40–60 — Visible development with substantial room to build.
DBeginner · 25–39 — Limited development visible in the submitted photo.
EStarting out · 0–24 — Little development visible to the model in this angle.

“Experienced” and “Beginner” are names for bands; a photo cannot verify training history. A single angle cannot establish whole-body balance when regions are hidden.

What the grade cannot establish

A tier is an AI visual opinion, not a muscle-mass measurement, health assessment, strength test or evidence of substance use. Lighting, pose, pump, clothing, framing and model variability can affect the result.

Use visible-muscle feedback as a possible focus, alongside your own training record. There is no validated number of years needed to reach a tier and no guarantee that a particular training or diet change will move the grade. Adults 18+ only.

Questions

What tier is my physique?
CutRank can offer an AI visual grade using its own S–E rubric. The result applies to the submitted photo and visible regions, not a universal ranking of your physique.
What tier is the average gym goer?
We do not have a representative population study that assigns an average gym-goer tier. CutRank's grade labels are product conventions, not measured population categories.
Does the same photo always get the same grade?
An AI interpretation can vary. Different lighting, pose and framing add uncertainty, so do not treat small score changes as measured physical progress.
How do I move up a tier?
Review the visible feedback alongside your goals and training record. A tier does not prescribe a programme or guarantee the effect of any training or diet change.
