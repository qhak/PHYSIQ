What A-tier is intended to describe

A is labelled “Advanced”: strong visible development within CutRank's scale. It sits below S, but that distinction is a product assessment, not a scientific boundary between physiques.

CutRank returns scores for visible muscle groups and a suggested focus. Review which regions are actually visible before drawing conclusions about proportions. A front photo does not establish what your back or legs look like.

The difference between A and S

The grade boundaries are 75–89 for A and 90–100 for S. Those bands explain the labels; they do not make the underlying visual assessment exact. No single weak point, shoulder ratio or body-fat percentage guarantees either grade.

CutRank's S–E rubric

These are CutRank's internal 0–100 grade bands. The labels are product shorthand for visible development, not clinical categories or measured population percentiles. The card's 1–10 comparison score is a separate product scale.

SElite · 90–100 — Exceptional visible development within CutRank's scale.
AAdvanced · 75–89 — Strong visible development, one band below S.
BExperienced · 61–74 — A developed visible base with areas to improve.
CDeveloping · 40–60 — Visible development with substantial room to build.
DBeginner · 25–39 — Limited development visible in the submitted photo.
EStarting out · 0–24 — Little development visible to the model in this angle.

“Experienced” and “Beginner” are names for bands; a photo cannot verify training history. A single angle cannot establish whole-body balance when regions are hidden.

What the grade cannot establish

A tier is an AI visual opinion, not a muscle-mass measurement, health assessment, strength test or evidence of substance use. Lighting, pose, pump, clothing, framing and model variability can affect the result.

Use visible-muscle feedback as a possible focus, alongside your own training record. There is no validated number of years needed to reach a tier and no guarantee that a particular training or diet change will move the grade. Adults 18+ only.

Is A-tier or S-tier a scientific classification?
No. These labels belong to CutRank's internal physique rubric. They do not establish health, strength, training history or population rank.
Can a tier tell whether someone is natural?
No. A physique photo or tier cannot establish substance use. The rubric describes visible appearance rather than how it was achieved.
How long does it take to reach this tier?
There is no validated timeline. The result depends on what the photo shows and the model's interpretation; a grade cannot verify years of training.
