Where A sits on the scale

A is CutRank's second-highest grade. It describes substantial visible muscular development, while S is reserved for exceptional development at the top of the same scale. These are CutRank grading labels, not scientific or population rankings.

See the complete S–E rubric for all bands and a photo-based self-check.

What A-tier actually looks like

  • Obvious in clothing. Shoulder width and upper back development read through a shirt — you don't have to be lean or flexing for it to show.
  • Development across the board. Shoulders, chest, back, arms and legs are all clearly trained. Nothing is untouched.
  • Real muscular size. Not just visible outlines from being lean, but genuine thickness and fullness in the muscle itself.
  • Visible differences between muscle groups. An A result may include areas that appear more developed than others. Which areas are visible depends on the photo angle and framing.

The gaps on either side

B → A

More total muscle

The gap below you is mostly size. B-tier has a visible base with clear weak points; A-tier has closed enough of them that development reads as complete rather than partial.

Muscle development takes sustained training, but a photo grade cannot predict an individual's timeline. Training history, nutrition, recovery and individual response all matter.

A → S

Removing the weak point

Differences between A- and S-tier in this rubric include overall development and how balanced the visible muscle groups appear. A single photo cannot establish that one lagging group is the reason for a grade.

Am I A-tier?

Self-assessment can shift with the comparison group, familiarity with your own appearance and photo conditions. Treat a tier as a description within this rubric, not a population ranking or an objective measure of progress.

An outside read helps. CutRank grades one photo from S to E, scores every visible muscle group, and names the weakest one. It doesn't flatter, which is the point — at A-tier, the flattering answer is the useless one.

What is an A-tier physique?

What is the difference between A-tier and S-tier?
Within CutRank's rubric, S is the highest grade and A is the grade below it. Their descriptions differ by degree of visible development, size and proportion. These are editorial photo-grading labels, not population rankings, and a photo may not show every area clearly.
How long does it take to reach A-tier?
There is no fixed timeline. CutRank's A grade describes visible development in a photo and does not encode how long someone has trained. Muscle gain varies with the individual, their starting point and their training.
