FFMI & lean mass · CutRank guide

Lean body mass is not the same as muscle mass

Lean body mass and muscle mass are different labels. In CutRank's calculator, lean body mass means estimated fat-free mass: body weight minus estimated fat mass. That total includes much more than skeletal muscle. A DXA report may instead label its bone-mineral-free soft-tissue compartment as lean mass, so check the report's definition before comparing it with a calculator or another device.

Read the label before comparing the number

A body-composition result is only useful if you know what it includes. This page uses fat-free mass for the mass remaining after estimated fat mass is subtracted from body weight. The CutRank lean body mass calculator uses this practical definition. It does not isolate the kilograms of skeletal muscle in your body.

DXA reporting adds another distinction: its components include fat, fat-free soft tissue and bone mineral. The lean soft-tissue result excludes the separate bone-mineral component. Research evaluating DXA explicitly reports these as different measurements. Source: regional body-composition DXA study.

Skeletal muscle is a tissue compartment within the broader non-fat total. A result called muscle mass should therefore identify how it was estimated. Do not assume that a calculator, scan and smart scale are measuring the same compartment simply because their numbers appear in kilograms.

An 80 kg example: what the subtraction actually gives you

Suppose someone weighs 80 kg and has an estimated body-fat percentage of 20%. Their calculated fat mass is 80 × 0.20 = 16 kg. Subtracting that leaves 64 kg of estimated fat-free mass.

It would be incorrect to report that result as 64 kg of skeletal muscle. The calculation has only divided body weight into fat and everything else. It has no separate input for muscle, organs, water or mineral, so it cannot tell you how the non-fat total is distributed.

If this person's height is 1.80 m, their raw FFMI is 64 ÷ 3.24 = 19.75. That index inherits the same definition: fat-free mass relative to height squared. Renaming it a muscle score would not change what went into it. Use the FFMI versus BMI comparison to see how this differs from using total body weight.

Why DXA lean tissue and calculated fat-free mass can differ

Consider a deliberately simplified DXA-style breakdown: 16 kg fat, 61 kg lean soft tissue and 3 kg bone mineral content. The components sum to 80 kg. For this example, the non-fat total is 61 + 3 = 64 kg, while the lean soft-tissue value remains 61 kg.

The 3 kg difference is explained by the component labels; it does not show a disagreement about muscle gain. Bone mineral content is also a specific measurement, not the complete weight of all bone tissue. Check the actual report's terminology and rounding before applying the example to your scan.

DXA-derived muscle estimates require another step. Kim and colleagues developed prediction models connecting appendicular lean soft tissue with skeletal muscle measured by MRI. That distinction matters: a useful relationship between two measurements does not make them identical. Source: Kim et al. (2002). Avoid converting total lean soft tissue to muscle with an arbitrary fixed percentage.

Make progress comparisons with matching measurements

An increase in measured lean mass is not automatically the same amount of new muscle protein. In an experiment involving trained male cyclists, changes in glycogen, creatine and water altered DXA lean-mass estimates over a short period. Source: Bone et al. (2017).

  • Keep the same metric: compare fat-free mass with fat-free mass, or lean soft tissue with lean soft tissue.
  • Keep the method visible: record whether the result came from a calculation, scan or device estimate.
  • Follow a consistent measurement protocol: note conditions that could make one reading unlike another.
  • Record the outcome you care about: use training records for performance and consistent measurements or photos for changes in size or appearance.

For example, a log entry saying 64 kg estimated fat-free mass, from 80 kg body weight and 20% estimated fat, is much easier to interpret than 64 kg muscle. If the next estimate changes, you can inspect the inputs before deciding what the change means. A clear label gives you a better starting point than an extra decimal place.

Common questions

Is lean body mass the same as fat-free mass?
They are often used interchangeably in fitness calculators, including CutRank's practical calculation. Technical definitions and report labels can differ. Check what the specific method includes, especially whether bone mineral is separate.
Can I calculate skeletal muscle from weight and body fat?
Those inputs allow a two-part estimate of fat mass and fat-free mass. They do not identify skeletal muscle separately, so subtracting fat from weight cannot supply a muscle-mass result.
Does losing lean mass on a reading mean I lost muscle?
It does not establish that by itself. Lean-mass measurements can change with water and glycogen conditions, and the total includes more than skeletal muscle. Compare repeated, consistently obtained readings with other progress records.
