The 25 figure, examined

Is 25 FFMI really the natural limit?

No — not as a hard ceiling. 25 is the figure most often cited as an approximate upper range for drug-free lifters, and it comes from a single 1995 study comparing steroid users with non-users. It describes where most drug-free lifters in that sample topped out. It was never a biological limit, and it can't classify an individual.

Where the number came from

In 1995, Kouri and colleagues published a study comparing the fat-free mass index of male athletes who used anabolic steroids against those who didn't. The drug-free group clustered below an FFMI of roughly 25, while the steroid-using group frequently exceeded it.

That's the entire origin of the number. It has since been repeated so many times that it's often stated as though it were a law of physiology — but what the study reported was a pattern in one sample of people, measured once, in the mid-nineties.

Which doesn't make it useless. As a rough reference for what drug-free training tends to produce, it holds up reasonably well, and it's a genuinely helpful sanity check against the physiques sold as natural online. The problem is what people do with it next.

What it can't tell you

The common misuse is treating 25 as a test: this person is above it, therefore they're using something. That reasoning doesn't survive contact with how the number is produced.

  • A group average can't classify an individual. Finding that most drug-free lifters fall below a line says nothing definitive about any specific person above it. Distributions have tails, and people at the tail are exactly who ends up competing.
  • It depends entirely on a body fat estimate. FFMI is calculated from fat-free mass, which is calculated from body fat percentage — a number almost nobody measures accurately. See below for how much that moves things.
  • Height distorts it. Lean mass doesn't scale with height squared, so raw FFMI quietly favours taller lifters. The normalization adjustment exists precisely because of this, and plenty of published FFMI figures don't apply it.
  • Genetic variation is real and large. Frame width, muscle belly length and insertion points all change how much lean mass a body carries at the same height.

How much a bad body fat guess moves it

This is the part that undermines most confident FFMI arguments. Take an 85 kg lifter at 1.80 m and change only the body fat estimate:

85 kg · 1.80 m · FFMI at different body fat estimates
20% BF21.0
17% BF21.8
14% BF22.6
11% BF23.3
8% BF24.1

Same person, same weight, same height — and the FFMI swings by more than three points purely on the body fat figure you feed it. Being wrong by five percentage points on body fat is completely ordinary when people are eyeballing it, and that's more than enough to move someone across the line in either direction.

Which is why an argument that rests on someone's FFMI usually rests, underneath, on a stranger's guess about their body fat.

Questions

Is 25 FFMI the natural limit?
No single number is a hard limit. 25 is the figure most often cited as an approximate upper range for drug-free lifters, based on a 1995 study by Kouri and colleagues comparing steroid users and non-users. It describes where most drug-free lifters in that sample topped out — not a biological ceiling that applies to every individual.
Can a natural lifter exceed 25 FFMI?
Yes. Particularly tall lifters, those with exceptional genetics, and anyone whose body fat percentage has been underestimated. Because FFMI is calculated from a body fat estimate, an error of a few percentage points is enough to push a number above or below 25 on its own.
Does an FFMI above 25 prove someone uses steroids?
No, and using it that way misreads the research. The original study reported a pattern across a group, not a test that classifies one person. Body fat measurement error, height, frame size and genetic variation all move an individual's FFMI enough to make it unreliable as evidence about anybody.
What is a realistic FFMI for a natural lifter?
Most trained, drug-free lifters land between 20 and 23. Reaching 23 to 25 generally reflects years of consistent training with favourable genetics. Above 25 is uncommon drug-free but not impossible — and the figure is only ever as reliable as the body fat estimate behind it.
What's the difference between FFMI and normalized FFMI?
Normalized FFMI adjusts for height. Lean mass scales faster than height squared, so the raw figure quietly favours taller lifters; the normalization adds a correction for anyone under 1.8 m. The 25 reference figure is usually discussed in normalized terms, which is one more reason two people quoting FFMI numbers can be talking past each other.
