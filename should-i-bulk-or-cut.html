What each option is trying to do

  • Gaining phase (bulk): a period intended to support increases in body size and muscle alongside training. The label does not guarantee that added weight is muscle.
  • Fat-loss phase (cut): a period intended to reduce body fat. Whether a deficit is appropriate requires more context than an appearance score.
  • Maintenance: keeping weight broadly stable while building a clearer record of training, recovery and progress. You do not have to choose a bulk or cut immediately.

A practical decision checklist

  • Name your goal. More strength, visible development and a different appearance are related but distinct goals.
  • Review a trend. Compare weight and training records over time, rather than one weigh-in or your best-lit photo.
  • Check recovery and feasibility. Consider energy, sleep, training demands and whether your current routine is sustainable. A photo cannot assess these.
  • Get individual guidance when needed. A registered dietitian can help decide whether changing intake fits your circumstances. A score is not a prescription.

Why we do not use automatic body-fat cutoffs

The old shortcut of “below this percentage, bulk; above it, cut” makes uncertain measurements look more decisive than they are. Estimated body fat does not capture your health, training demands, preferences or history.

The IOC's body-composition recommendations emphasize health and the risks of overfocusing on leanness. They do not validate CutRank or supply a universal bulk/cut threshold.

Health comes before a phase label

Persistent fatigue, dizziness, menstrual changes or deteriorating performance are reasons to seek qualified advice, not to intensify a diet because of a photo score. The 2023 IOC REDs consensus discusses how problematic low energy availability can affect health and performance.

Questions

Should I bulk or cut first?
A body-fat estimate alone cannot decide. Consider your goal, recent training and weight trends, recovery, and whether changing intake is appropriate for you. Maintaining weight while reviewing those inputs is also an option.
Can a photo tell me whether to bulk or cut?
A photo can show visible development and definition under particular conditions. It cannot measure energy needs, health or recovery. CutRank's visual feedback should not be the sole basis for a diet decision.
Do I need a precise body-fat number?
No single percentage settles this question. Different assessment methods and conditions can produce different estimates. A small estimated change should not automatically trigger a new diet phase.
What if I am lean but do not look muscular?
Appearance alone does not establish what your calorie intake should be. Review your training progression, goals and recovery before choosing a gaining or dieting phase; discuss uncertainty with a qualified dietitian.
How long should a bulk or cut last?
There is no universal timeline. Review trends over a planned period with appropriate guidance rather than changing direction after each photo or daily weight fluctuation.
