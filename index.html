01 · Published rubric

The scale isn't a secret

Every scan is judged against these bands — the same ones written into the grading instructions. Nothing is tuned per user, and nobody gets a softer scale for paying.

The model commits to a band before it writes a single number, so the letter on your card and the scores behind it can't drift apart.

Your card shows a 1–10 score instead, given twice: once against everyone, once against people who actually train. One point is one standard deviation, and the gym scale is the harder of the two by design.

S90–100Elite — mass far beyond a typical trained lifter
A75–89Advanced — years of obvious hard training
B61–74Experienced — solid base, real weak points
C40–60Developing — visible training effect, modest overall
D25–39Beginner — minimal visible development
E0–24No visible training adaptation yet
02 · It says no

Bad photos get refused, not flattered

Under-18, explicit, multi-person, blurry, dark, or badly cropped photos are rejected instead of guessed at — and a refused scan doesn't spend your free scan. A tool that grades everything you feed it isn't reading anything.

It also can't see what a photo can't show: training history, substances, injuries, or health. Those limits are stated on the result, not buried.

03 · Same read, every angle

Size and leanness are scored separately

Muscle size is judged on frame width, projection, insertions, and limb girth — cues body fat can't fake. Leanness is scored on its own line as conditioning. That's why a soft big physique and a lean small one don't collapse into the same middling number.

Each angle is only scored for what it can actually see. A front photo never returns a back score.

What CutRank can and can't judge.

It grades what the photo gives it — nothing more.

01 · Development

Visible muscle development

CutRank looks for visible size, shape, separation, and how much trained tissue is actually readable in the image. If a muscle is hidden, it should not get a confident score.

02 · Shape

Proportion and symmetry

It compares what is visible across the frame: upper to lower, front to back, left to right, and whether one area is carrying the whole photo while another is lagging.

03 · Conditioning

Conditioning, not diagnosis

Leanness, definition, and softness affect the read. Body fat is shown only as a rough visual estimate, not a body composition measurement and not medical truth.

04 · Readability

Photo quality limits

Lighting, pose, pump, camera angle, cropping, blur, clothing, and body fat can all change the result. Bad inputs get a bad read, or no read at all.

The "top %" on a result is a modelled estimate, not a measured ranking — there is no audited population behind it, so it is labelled an AI estimate everywhere it appears. Scores describe how this photo reads to the model, nothing more. Treat the output as directional feedback, then go lift.

Straight answers.

Is the free scan really free?
Yes. One angle, fully graded, no card required. Just an email to reveal the result.
Why do you need my email?

What photo works best?
Good light, plain background, shorts, full region in frame. A phone camera is fine. Bad photos get refused — they don't get graded generously.
Can I cancel anytime?
Yes. No contracts, no questions.
How accurate is the grading?
CutRank uses an AI vision model to estimate visible muscle development from the photo you upload. It is not a medical tool, a DEXA scan, or a body-composition measurement.
Are the numbers on this page real?
Yes, and they're the only numbers here. The scan and physique counts come from the grading service's own counters, rounded down and shown with a "+". There are no testimonials, star ratings, "as seen in" logos, or user quotes on this site, because there is nothing to show that wouldn't be invented.
Does the same photo always get the same grade?
Close, but not identical. Every scan runs against the same published rubric and the model commits to a grade band before it scores individual muscles, which keeps results tight — but an AI read still varies slightly, and a different pose, light, or pump moves it more than the model does.
What happens to my photo?
It is sent to the AI provider for the scan and is not stored by CutRank. We do not use your photos to train CutRank. The provider may process the image under its own terms to return the result.
Does it work for women?
It can assess adult physiques of any gender from photos, but results depend heavily on pose, lighting, clothing, and what is visible. Treat it as directional feedback, not a definitive ranking.
What does a C grade actually mean?
C means the photo shows a visible training base, but also obvious work left. It does not mean you're impressive from every angle — that's B and above. It's an honest baseline, not an insult.
Does CutRank only grade natural lifters?
No. CutRank only sees the photo. It cannot detect substance use, training history, injuries, or health status.
How is this different from asking a PT or posting on Reddit?
A coach can understand context and help you plan. CutRank is faster and more direct: it gives a consistent AI read of the photo, with the limits of photo-based AI clearly stated.
Who processes my photo?
The image goes to Anthropic's AI model for grading, under its own terms. Stripe handles payments. Nobody else touches it.
Is this medical advice?
No. It's a visual read of one photo — not a diagnosis, a body-composition scan, or a health tool.
What if the AI is wrong?
It can be. Lighting, pose, and photo quality change the result. Treat it as directional feedback, not a verdict on you.
Can minors use it?
